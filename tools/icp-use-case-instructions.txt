ICP AND USE-CASE DIFFERENTIATION
Build candidate ICP operating contexts, then ask what changes the product's actual job across People, Process, Technology and Input. These are independent segmentation axes, not four compulsory maturity ladders. Industry, department, role and country are categorical differences; a use case may exist at several company sizes or only where the relevant activity exists. Headcount does not prove maturity. For payroll, distinguish HR/payroll staff (People) from employees whose pay is processed (Input).

Use only meaningful optional subcolumns. Input may vary by size/volume, workforce mix, geography and data completeness/timeliness; add quality only when it changes the workflow. Process may vary by industry, department/function, jurisdiction or pay model. People may vary by operator, approver, buyer role and expertise. Technology may vary by the buyer's existing HRIS, attendance, accounting/ERP and integration or migration requirements. Validate each split against the product and market; do not force every axis to have subcolumns, invent differences, or imply all industries must pass through the same stages.

Use the nested table structure: an axis can stand alone, or contain a dimension and named members, e.g. Process > Industries > Manufacturing / SaaS. Put the actual changed workflow or integration requirement in each cell, not just repeat the member's name. Add explicit use-case/page angles where useful, e.g. Payroll software for SMBs or Attendance-linked payroll for manufacturers. Page ideas are hypotheses, not created Keywords pages or verified search demand. Allow sparse rows and blank inapplicable cells; never repeat text merely to fill the grid. Staff counts and size bands are illustrative, not legal definitions or compulsory buying thresholds.

For each candidate, include a concrete buying trigger and distinguish current practice, required capability and any proposed future workflow. In View More, add diagrams only when they clarify a process; progression means real changes in how that same process is carried out, not different industries disguised as maturity. Do not infer native integrations, supported countries, staffing ratios, customer results or superiority from the proposed architecture. Verify named compatibility before making integration-page claims. Keep sources in internal research metadata.

When mapping options before the user's choice, leave selectedIcp empty and do not finalize a use-case stage. Preserve other positioning sections, selections, products and Keywords unless explicitly included. Save the active product's table configuration and cells together: icpTableColumns and icpCustomColumns define dimensions; icpNestedColumns defines member IDs; row.dimensions[axis][JSON.stringify([dimensionId, memberId])] stores nested cells. Flat dimension cells use their dimension ID; unsplit axes use row.people/process/technology/input. Retain existing IDs and recoverable prior content when revising, update the editable AI prompt, use a revision guard, and read back both active tabs and the product workspace.
